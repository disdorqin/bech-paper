# Allowed and Forbidden Claims

> Part of HCH v2 Derived Loss Math Design v0.1

## 16.1 Allowed Claims (after evidence is established)

The following sentences may be used in the abstract, introduction, or methods,
**provided they are supported by S3 evidence from the empirical audit**:

### Distribution and Modeling

1. "We model frozen-host electricity price residuals using a state-conditioned
   Fernandez-Steel skew-t distribution, where the continuous state captures
   market context."

2. "The conditional distribution provides both directional occurrence probability
   and conditional expected magnitude, eliminating the need for separate
   classification and regression heads."

3. "The skew-t family nests symmetric Student-t and normal distributions as
   special cases, enabling controlled ablation of asymmetry and heavy tails."

### Decision and Routing

4. "Up and Down correction candidates are derived as directional partial moments
   of the fitted conditional distribution."

5. "A day-episodic memory (CAGM) retrieves historical action gains, and a
   value-based router (DVG) selects among Identity, Down, and Up actions
   based on risk-adjusted value."

6. "The correction magnitude is calibrated on a disjoint validation segment (S3)
   using a safety constraint that bounds expected harm in normal periods."

### Empirical (tentative)

7. "On [N] public electricity markets, the FS skew-t conditional distribution
   achieves lower held-out negative log-likelihood than the symmetric Student-t
   baseline (p < 0.05, block-bootstrap)."

8. "The distribution-derived bidirectional candidates, combined with CAGM-DVG
   routing, achieve [X]% MAE reduction on extreme-price episodes with [Y]%
   increase in normal-period error (S3 metrics)."

9. "Ablation confirms that removing the skew parameter degrades tail calibration
   without improving normal-period performance."

### Cross-Market (only if validated)

10. "A shared backbone trained on source markets with market-specific bias terms
    transfers to held-out target markets with [Z]% of the performance gain of
    market-specific training."

## 16.2 Forbidden Claims

The following MUST NOT appear in any paper draft, abstract, or claim list:

### Novelty Inflation

- "We propose a novel Student-t-based loss function" — Student-t NLL is not novel
- "This is the first use of skew-t in electricity price forecasting" — false;
  skew-t has been used in EPF literature (e.g., Marcjasz+ 2022)
- "The Fernandez-Steel skew-t distribution is our contribution" — FS (1998) is
  a standard statistical method
- "TFT and N-BEATS demonstrated the effectiveness of Student-t likelihood" —
  they did not; TFT uses quantile loss, N-BEATS uses sMAPE

### Distribution Claims (without S4 evidence)

- "Host residuals follow an asymmetric Student-t distribution" — this is a
  model assumption, not a verified fact
- "The partial moment is Bayes-optimal" — not proven for the 3-action problem
- "A single distribution unifies spike, negative-price, and normal-period modeling"
- "The analytic NLL is strictly superior to composite losses"

### Generalization Claims (without S4 evidence)

- "Model-agnostic" — only established if tested across Linear through PatchTST
- "Cross-market zero-shot" — only if NO target-market information is used
- "Reaches state-of-the-art" — requires S4 public benchmark comparison
- "Zero degradation on normal periods" — requires statistical demonstration,
  not just visual inspection
- "First calibrated correction-action risk" — CRC (Yao+ 2024) has safety constraints

### Private Data Claims

- "Shandong results prove..." — private data cannot be the sole evidence for
  a public claim
- Any claim where the primary supporting evidence is from Shandong private data

## 16.3 Required Caveats

Every paper draft MUST include:

1. **Distribution caveat**: "The FS skew-t is a modeling choice; we do not claim
   that residuals are generated by a skew-t process. Any parametric distribution
   is an approximation."

2. **Candidate caveat**: "Partial-moment candidates are heuristic corrections;
   they are not established as Bayes-optimal for the three-action problem."

3. **Freeze caveat**: "All HCH parameters and distribution choices were fixed
   before S4 evaluation. No S4 data informed any modeling decision."

4. **Data caveat**: "Shandong data motivated the problem formulation and informed
   method design but is not the primary reproducible evidence. Public-market
   results are independently verifiable."

## 16.4 How to Frame the Contribution

**Recommended framing** (after evidence):

> "We present a frozen-host post-processing framework that derives bidirectional
> correction actions from a single state-conditioned heavy-tailed residual
> distribution, combined with episodic memory-based action routing. The
> approach is evaluated on [N] public electricity markets with [M] frozen
> forecasters."

This framing:
- Does NOT claim distribution novelty
- Does NOT claim SOTA forecasting
- Claims the architecture (distribution -> candidates -> memory routing) as
  the contribution
- Makes testable evaluation claims

**Alternative framing if evidence is weak**:

> "We investigate whether a state-conditioned skew-t residual model, when
> coupled with episodic action-value routing, can improve selective extreme-price
> correction in electricity markets. Our experiments find [mixed/positive/negative]
> evidence."

This is acceptable if the evidence does not support stronger claims. An honest
negative result is better than an unsupported positive claim.
